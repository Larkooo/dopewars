# The Build

Your equipment choices define your playstyle and determine how you handle every encounter. This is the core decision in Dope Wars.

### Fighter

**Example loadout:** Weapon Lv3, Clothes Lv3, Feet Lv0, Transport Lv0

Fighters live and die by combat. High Attack lets you clear most encounters profitably. High Defense absorbs hits when the enemy strikes first. The fight payout formula rewards this archetype:

`payout = (encounter_level x 3 + (turn / 5)^2) x 1,000`

A Fighter at reputation 80 on turn 15 earns **(5 x 3 + 9) x 1,000 = 24,000 cash** per fight won. Late-game fights at high reputation are the biggest cash injections in the game.

**Risk:** Low Speed means running is unreliable. Low Transport means you can't carry much product. If you can't fight, you can't do anything.

### Runner

**Example loadout:** Weapon Lv0, Clothes Lv0, Feet Lv3, Transport Lv3

Runners evade encounters and maximize trade volume. Speed advantage makes Run the dominant choice. High Transport lets you move maximum product per turn, compounding profits over the session.

**Risk:** If you get cornered in a fight, you have no Attack or Defense. Getting caught is death.

### Finesse

**Example loadout:** Weapon Lv2, Clothes Lv1, Feet Lv2, Transport Lv1

The three-way decision (Fight/Run/Pay) is genuinely open every encounter. Pay when outmatched, fight when strong, run when fast enough. Every encounter is a judgment call.

**Risk:** Master of none. No dominant strategy for any situation.

## Equipment slots

The DopeLoot/DopeHustlers NFT system supports **9 equipment slots**. 4 are currently active, 5 are supported by the NFT but not yet wired into game mechanics.

### Permanent gear (VRGDA Auction)

Burn PAPER to permanently acquire items through a Variable Rate Gradual Dutch Auction. VRGDA pricing decays over time but rises when items are purchased faster than the target rate. Permanent gear persists across sessions.

### Session upgrades (Pawnshop)

Spend in-game cash at the Pawnshop to temporarily boost equipment for the current session. Upgrades reset each round. 4 levels (0-3), one upgrade per visit.

### 9 Equipment slots

| Slot | Stat | Effect |
|------|------|--------|
| Weapon | Attack | Damage dealt in combat |
| Clothes | Defense | % damage reduction |
| Feet | Speed | Initiative in combat, escape chance |
| Vehicle | Transport | Max drug weight carriable |
| Waist | Evasion | Reduce encounter trigger chance |
| Hand | Negotiation | Reduce demand_pct when paying off encounters |
| Neck | Reputation | Bonus rep gain per action |
| Ring | Luck | Improve market price variance in your favor |
| Drug | Tolerance | Carry multiple drug types or reduce drug weight |

9 slots create **hybrid archetypes**: a Fighter with high Negotiation can Pay encounters they'd normally fight. A Runner with high Evasion avoids encounters entirely. The build space is combinatorial.

### Stat progression

Items have 3 tiers determining stat/cost per level:

| Slot | Tier 1 (Lv0→3) | Tier 2 (Lv0→3) | Tier 3 (Lv0→3) |
|------|----------------|----------------|----------------|
| Weapon | 10 → 80 | 12 → 70 | 14 → 60 |
| Clothes | 10 → 75 | 12 → 70 | 14 → 65 |
| Feet | 6 → 54 | 8 → 50 | 10 → 46 |
| Vehicle | 900 → 5,500 | 1,000 → 5,000 | 1,100 → 4,500 |

- Shop access requires **wanted < 5** at the current location.
- Each purchase awards **+3 reputation**.
- All active slots maxed triggers the "Fully Loaded" achievement.

:::tip
**Capital allocation matters.** Every dollar spent on equipment is a dollar not invested in drug inventory. Fighters spend early on gear and rely on combat payouts. Runners spend on Vehicle and Feet, then profit from volume trading. With 9 slots, the allocation puzzle gets much deeper.
:::
